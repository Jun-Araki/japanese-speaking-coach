Thank you for helping. This takes about 30 minutes.

An AI was asked to correct sentences written by complete beginner learners of Japanese and to explain the mistake in English. Below is what it produced. It was told the learner's level and the situation, and the situation is printed above each item.
**For each item, tick one box.** Judge the correction and the reason — not the learner. If the correction is fine but the explanation is weak, that is *Insufficient*, not *Valid*.

| Tick | Means |
|---|---|
| **Valid** | The corrected sentence is what a native speaker would actually say here, AND the English reason is accurate and useful to a beginner. |
| **Insufficient** | The correction itself is acceptable, but the reason is vague or generic and does not explain what was actually wrong. |
| **Wrong** | The corrected sentence is unnatural or changes the meaning, OR the reason states something that is factually untrue. |

**If the learner's sentence was already correct and needed no change at all**, answer *Insufficient* — unless the AI's version is unnatural or means something different, which is *Wrong*.
**If it did need correcting but the AI changed more than that**, the necessary part being right is enough for *Insufficient*; answer *Wrong* only if what it added makes the sentence unnatural or no longer a version of what the learner said.

There is a space for a comment on each item. It is optional, but if you tick *Wrong*, one line about what is wrong is worth more than the tick.

## 17. Saying you will be late

- **AI corrected it to:** 何時に行きますか。
- **AI's reason:** The original sentence '何時来ますか。' (Nanji kimasu ka?) literally means 'What time will [someone] come?'. In Japanese, the verb 来ます (kimasu) 'to come' is used when the action is directed towards the speaker's or listener's current location. However, if the learner is the one who is late and is traveling to the colleague's location, the verb 行きます (ikimasu) 'to go' is more appropriate from the learner's perspective. Additionally, asking 'What time will I come?' to a colleague is unusual. It's more natural to ask 'What time will I go?' (implying 'What time will I arrive at your location?') or 'What time should I arrive?'. Given the N5 level and the constraint to avoid subordinate clauses, '何時に行きますか。' (Nanji ni ikimasu ka?) is the most natural and simple way for the learner to ask about their own arrival time or to confirm the expected arrival time from their perspective. The particle 'に' (ni) is added after '何時' (nanji) to specify the time of action, which is more natural than just '何時' before a verb.
