# ています — a state that holds right now

「〜ています」 does two jobs: an action in progress, and a state that began earlier and
still holds.

- バス**が**遅れ**ています**。 (The bus is running late — right now.)
- 大阪に住ん**でいます**。 (I live in Osaka — and still do.)
- ピアノを習っ**ています**。 (I am learning the piano.)
- 心から感謝し**ています**。 (I am grateful.)

## Plain ます often points at the future

This is the difference beginners most often make without knowing it:

- 父は医者をし**ます**。 → sounds like a plan: he will become one.
- 父は医者をし**ています**。 → what he does now.

Both are correct Japanese. Only one of them answers "what does he do?".

## Neither form is wrong on its own

The same pair with waiting, where both readings are useful:

- 待ち**ます**。 (I will wait — a promise, in answer to being told someone is late.)
- 待っ**ています**。 (I am waiting — describing the situation right now.)

Which one is right depends on what the speaker meant. This is not a sentence to
correct on sight, and neither is 「行きます」 against 「行っています」.

## The bare stem does not end a sentence

「遅れ」 is a piece of a verb, not a verb. A sentence has to finish:

- ✗ バスが遅れ。 → ✓ バスが遅れ**ています**。

Compare 「遅れます」, which says *you* will be late — the right choice when telephoning
ahead about yourself: 「すみません、少し遅れます。」

## Negatives

The polite negative of this form is 「〜ていません」:

- まだ終わっ**ていません**。 (It isn't finished yet.)

Dropping to 「終わっていない」 is a decision about politeness rather than about grammar,
and it depends entirely on who is listening — see grammar-008. For the 「する」 verbs
running through this document, see grammar-005.
