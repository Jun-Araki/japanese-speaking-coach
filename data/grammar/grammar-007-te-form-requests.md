# The て-form: asking, thanking, and who is doing what

## 〜てください — please do this

- ちょっと待っ**てください**。 (Please wait a moment.)
- ケンと呼ん**でください**。 (Please call me Ken.)

Dropping 「ください」 leaves the casual request 「待って」. Between friends that is
normal; to someone you have just met it is below the floor. It is not a command —
the command is 「待て」 — but it is far too close for a stranger.

Watch who the request points at. 「私は」 in front makes *you* the one doing the
calling, which is the opposite of what was meant:

- ✗ 私はケンと呼んでください。 → ✓ ケンと呼んでください。

## To a manager, 「ください」 is not the top of the ladder

「ください」 still tells the listener to do something. Asking a manager for their
action goes one step further, to 「〜ていただけますか」 or the honorific 「お〜ください」:

- to a shop assistant: ちょっと見せ**てください**。
- to your manager: ちょっと**お見せいただけますか**。／**お見せください**。

The prefix is 「お」 with native verbs (お待ちください、お読みください) and 「ご」 with
Chinese-derived nouns (ご確認ください). 「ご見せください」 does not exist.

This applies only to *their* action. Talking about your own, です・ます is enough —
「少し考えます」 needs nothing added. See grammar-008.

## 〜てくれてありがとう — thank you for doing that

Thanking someone for something they did uses the て-form plus 「くれて」:

- 送っ**てくれて**ありがとう。
- 教え**てくれて**ありがとう。

What is missing in 「私を助けて、ありがとう」 is the 「くれて」, not anything else —
「私を助けてくれてありがとう。」 is fine. 「私を」 is usually dropped because it is obvious, not
because it is wrong. And the verb has to reach the て-form first: 「手伝う、ありがとう」
has not.

## The two request forms point in opposite directions

This is the request mistake that changes the meaning rather than the politeness.

- 〜**てもいいですか** asks permission for something **you** will do.
  写真を撮っ**てもいいですか**。 (May I take a photo?)
- 〜**てもらえますか** asks the other person to do something **for you**.
  写真を撮っ**てもらえますか**。 (Could you take a photo for me?)

So 「これを見せてもいいですか」 asks the shop assistant to let *you* show *them*
something, when what you wanted was for them to show it to you.
