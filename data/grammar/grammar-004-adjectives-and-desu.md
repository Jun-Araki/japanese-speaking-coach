# Adjectives, and what です can and cannot carry

## い-adjectives carry their own tense

An い-adjective changes its own ending for the past. 「です」 is added afterwards only
to make the sentence polite — it cannot hold the tense itself.

- 忙しい → 忙しかった → 忙しかった**です** ✓
- ✗ 忙しい**でした**

「でした」 belongs to nouns and な-adjectives, which have no past form of their own:

- 元気**でした**。 (I was well.)
- 静か**でした**。 (It was quiet.)

## な-adjectives need な in front of a noun

- 大変**な**仕事 (a demanding job)
- 仕事は大変**でした**。 (The job was hard.)

「大変」 means demanding or troublesome, not large. 「大変な荷物」 says the bags were a
handful; after someone helps you carry them, what you say is 「荷物、大変でしたね。」

## Nouns state facts with です

Age and price are stated with 「です」, not with a verb:

- 弟は20歳**です**。
- チケットは千円**です**。

English "I have 20 years" becomes a sentence nobody says:

- ✗ 弟は20歳**を持っています**。

The same trap appears with lateness. 「遅い」 describes something that is slow;
arriving later than agreed is the verb 「遅れる」:

- ✗ 私は15分遅いです。 → ✓ 15分ほど遅れます。

(A job is different — you *do* it, so it takes a verb. See grammar-005.)

## です cannot be bolted onto a set phrase

Greetings and set expressions are single units. They are not assembled from parts,
so 「です」 cannot be added to make them polite:

- ✗ おはよう**です** → ✓ おはようございます
- ✗ お会計、お願い**です** → ✓ お会計、お願いします

「お願いです」 does exist in other shapes — 「一生のお願いです」 — but it is not the polite
form of 「お願い」, and it cannot stand where 「お願いします」 belongs. The set phrases are
collected in grammar-008.
