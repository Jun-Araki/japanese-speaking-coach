# Nouns that become verbs with する, and verbs that become nouns with こと

## A noun on its own is not yet a sentence

A large group of Japanese nouns becomes a verb by adding 「する」: 勉強、確認、感謝、
残業、予約、報告、説明。 Left alone they name a thing; they do not say that anyone did
it.

- ✗ 心から感謝。 → ✓ 心から感謝**しています**。
- ✗ 明日、予約。 → ✓ 明日、予約**します**。

## する or をする

Both shapes exist — 「掃除します」「掃除をします」 are equally ordinary. Use the shorter
one as soon as the sentence already carries an object with 「を」, because two 「を」 do
not sit together in one clause:

- ✓ 日本語**を**勉強します。
- ✗ 日本語**を**勉強**を**します。

## A job you hold is しています

- ✓ 父は医者をしています。 (what he does now)
- 父は医者をします。 → a plan for later: he is going to become one.

Both are correct Japanese; only the first answers "what do you do?". The difference
between 「します」 and 「しています」 is the subject of grammar-006.

## Sometimes the situation simply has its own word

「このシャツ、試すことができますか」 is correct Japanese, and a shop assistant would
understand it — but the word for trying clothes on is 「試着」, and a beginner is
better served by being told so:

- このシャツ、試着できますか。
- 「レジ袋」 or 「袋」, never 「プラスチックバッグ」.
- Choosing what to buy is 「これにします」, not 「これを取ります」.

Nothing here is a grammar error; the sentences are simply not what the shop says.
Under the labelling rule that still counts as needing correction, because a native
speaker would not phrase it that way.

## こと turns a verb back into a noun

「です」 cannot follow a plain-form verb. The verb has to be nominalised first:

- ✗ 趣味は絵を描くです。 → ✓ 趣味は絵を描く**こと**です。

(い-adjectives are the exception: they take です directly — 「寒いです」. See
grammar-004.)

The same pattern appears in 「〜たいことがあります」:

- 相談したいこと**が**あります。

The particle there is 「が」, because 「あります」 marks what exists — see grammar-002.
